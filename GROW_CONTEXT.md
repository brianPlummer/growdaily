## What to flag

Do not assume anything do not treat past experience as fact do research and show sources for knowledge. Write any and all assumptions into an assumptions section.

If you see anything in the image that suggests:
- Light stress (tacoing, bleaching, burnt tips)
- Nutrient issue (yellowing patterns, interveinal chlorosis, edge burn)
- Water issue (droop, claw, soggy/dry soil surface)
- Pest activity (chewed leaves, webbing, visible bugs, spots)
- Mold / disease (white powder, gray fluff, dark lesions)
- Growth stall (no new node development between snapshots)

…call it out clearly with specifics on which leaves/area, and suggest a corrective action.

If nothing stands out, say so; don't manufacture problems.

**Same-day test vs. real daily check-in.** Treat a comparison as a *test* ONLY when the two snapshots carry the **same calendar date** (I sometimes take several photos in one day while adjusting the setup). For a test, note that the images are only minutes or hours apart, so differences likely reflect camera angle or setup tweaks rather than plant change, then give an overall prognosis of the plant.

When the two snapshots fall on **different calendar dates, treat it as a normal day-over-day check-in and analyze it fully, even if fewer than 24 hours elapsed** (for example an afternoon photo vs. the next morning). Do NOT label it "same-day," "~18h," or dismiss it as a test based on the hours between shots. The calendar date is the only thing that decides this.
